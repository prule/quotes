# The Man Who Hated Tennis — And Became Its Greatest: Andre Agassi's Transformation

## A Sport He Never Chose

So much of Andre Agassi's life has been spent in the public eye — the highs and lows, the transformation from tennis brat to elder statesman — that it was possible to wonder how much more there was to be said. Plenty, it turns out.

The most startling revelation in his autobiography *Open* wasn't the drugs. It was this: Agassi had always hated tennis "with a dark and secret passion" because of his overbearing father.

Born on April 29, 1970, Agassi first picked up a racket when he was a toddler at the insistence of his father — an immigrant from Iran and a former Olympic boxer — who made Agassi practice for hours at the family's Las Vegas home. There was never a conversation about whether young Andre wanted this life. It was simply imposed on him.

In his mid-teens, Agassi abandoned his education to train full time, moving to Florida to attend the Nick Bollettieri Tennis Academy. He turned professional at sixteen. He'd never really had a childhood — just a racket and a father's ambition.

---

## The Star Who Had Everything and Felt Nothing

By his mid-twenties, Agassi had Wimbledon, a US Open, an Olympic gold medal, and the world's attention. He was ranked No. 1. He was one of the most recognisable athletes on the planet.

And he was utterly lost.

"You know, I was in a life that I didn't want to be in. I didn't want to be playing tennis either. So my life was filled with things I didn't want, things I didn't choose," Agassi said.

"I'm doing one of the most addictive recreational drugs that exists. I'm trying to harm myself. I hated my life. I hated it because I didn't know who I was. I didn't choose it."

When his assistant offered him a white powder — crystal meth — Agassi's decision was: "Why not? Can't feel any worse." He described "a sadness that came with it initially, followed by the energy and a chemically induced reconnection to life. I was looking for anything to make me get off the couch, to re-engage."

---

## Rock Bottom: Ranked 141st in the World

By late 1997, he had dropped to No. 141 in the rankings and was playing in tennis' equivalent of the minor leagues. The man who had been the face of tennis was now a cautionary tale — battling addiction, failing drug tests, and lying to the ATP about what had happened.

A doctor from the ATP called and informed him he had failed a drug test, was facing a three-month suspension, and needed to write a letter explaining himself. Agassi lied in the letter — claiming he had accidentally drunk from a spiked soda. The ATP accepted the explanation and threw the result out.

The shame, he later said, was profound.

---

## The Moment He Finally Chose His Own Life

Then something cracked open. Facing the abyss — ranked 141st, in a marriage he didn't want, addicted to a drug he'd turned to in desperation — Agassi made a decision that would change everything.

"This is my choice and my choice alone. And I made the decision right then and there that I'm gonna choose to fight this battle. And I'm gonna choose tennis," Agassi told interviewer Katie Couric. "Was that the first time in your life you felt it was your choice?" she asked. "It was the first time in my life. Twenty-seven years old. Ranked 141 in the world. And in a marriage that I shouldn't be in."

For the first time, tennis wasn't something being done to him. It was something he was choosing. The transformation that followed was staggering.

---

## The Greatest Comeback in Tennis History

He resuscitated his career in 1998, making the biggest one-year jump into the top 10 in the history of the ATP rankings. The next season, he won the French Open to complete a career Grand Slam, then added a second US Open title en route to finishing 1999 ranked No. 1 in the world.

The man who had been on crystal meth and ranked 141st was now, again, the best tennis player on the planet. But this time, it meant something. Because this time, it was his choice.

Eventually, Agassi reclaimed his form on the court, winning his final Grand Slam title at the 2003 Australian Open. He retired in 2006.

---

## The Life Beyond Tennis

The most remarkable part of the story isn't the comeback. It's what Agassi became after.

He raised tens of millions of dollars for at-risk youths in his hometown of Las Vegas and opened a preparatory academy there. The Andre Agassi College Preparatory School opened its doors in West Las Vegas in 2001, providing at-risk children with educational opportunities — a man who hated his own education building one for children who'd never had the chance.

He married Steffi Graf. He became a father. He found, for the first time, a life he had actually chosen.

"It fits with the story arc of his redemption," one observer noted. But the redemption wasn't for public consumption — it was deeply personal. The transformation wasn't from failure to success. It was from a life lived for others to a life finally lived for himself.

*"Getting to the end of myself. Staring across that abyss. Feeling so useless that a gust of wind would blow you off the edge."* That, Agassi said, was where the real journey began.
